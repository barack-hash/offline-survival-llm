GROUND-TO-AIR EMERGENCY CODE (Figure 19-6). This code is five definite, meaningful symbols. Make these symbols a minimum of 1 meter wide and 6 meters long. If you make them larger, keep the same 1:6 ratio. Ensure the signal contrasts greatly with the ground it is on. Place it in an open area easily spotted from the air.
1. Require assistance - symbol V
2. Require medical assistance - symbol X
3. No or negative - symbol N
4. Yes or affirmative - symbol Y
5. Proceed in this direction - symbol arrow (pointing the way to go)

BODY SIGNALS (Figure 19-7). When an aircraft is close enough for the pilot to see you clearly, use body movements or positions to convey a message. Captions exact; postures as drawn:
- Can proceed shortly; wait if practicable: standing, one arm stretched straight out to the side.
- Land here (point in direction of landing): crouching, both arms pointing forward in the direction of landing.
- Need mechanical help or parts; long delay: standing, both arms stretched straight out to the sides.
- All OK; do not wait: standing, one arm raised straight up.
- Do not attempt to land here: standing, both arms raised and waved back and forth over the head.
- Pick us up; aircraft abandoned: standing, both arms raised straight up.
- Use drop message: leaning to one side, one arm swung in an arc toward the ground.
- Affirmative (yes): standing, waving a cloth up and down beside the body.
- Negative (no): standing, waving a cloth from side to side across the body.
- Our receiver is operating: standing, both hands cupped over the ears.
- Need medical assistance urgently: lying flat on the ground.
